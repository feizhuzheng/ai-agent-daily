Reflection: Three things, and the first one reverses a conclusion rather than adding to it. The Reddit half of cross-round dedupe cannot be built the way it was filed, and this run proved why. The programmatic check found zero Reddit repeats; close reading found roughly seven. The reason is structural: published Ideas entries are English paraphrases, not source titles, so comparing this window's candidate titles against the text of the last three articles can never match. The capacitor-leg bending jig, the KeePass single-credential transfer, the quiet-accessible-places travel app, floor-board layout, the history learning app, the pickup basketball run and the action-figure arm swap were all originals from the last two editions, pulled back by the five-day window overlapping the previous ones, and caught only by memory. The enabling change is to store each idea's source identifier at publish time; that is the proposal filed this run, and the existing Reddit-dedupe proposal cannot be executed before it. Second, the Twitter side of Ideas did not fail the volume test it was set last run - 36 usable items against a threshold of fifteen - but it failed a test nobody had written down. Almost none of those 36 are statements of need. They are commentary about gaps, and they cluster on a handful of accounts: one person supplied three of them. Genuine "I want X and it does not exist" tweets numbered under ten in a 336-post window. The honest conclusion is not that Twitter is dead for this feed, it is that the division of labour changed: Twitter now supplies the thematic judgement and Reddit supplies the concrete product gaps, and the two demoted workhorse phrases turn out to be excellent at the former while being useless at the latter. Third, the substantive convergence is that agent requests stopped asking for capability and started asking for accountability. Six separate unmet needs this window were variations on the same thing - a replayable execution record rather than a forensic trace, an explicit contract for what counts as done, a runtime-enforceable rulebook for when an agent may act alone, silent-failure detection for cheap routers, an inspectable policy layer that survives updates, and an artifact recording who actually decided when a change passed through two agents and one human. The sharpest version came from someone who located the economics precisely: the hard part is not watching it work once, it is the second time, when you decide whether to check the output or trust it, and checking often costs more than doing the task yourself.

Ideas ran a separate getTwitterPostsByIds verification on all 38 tweet IDs with createdAtDate included - zero mismatches and all inside the window. Cross-task isolation was enforced programmatically on all three lines: Loop excluded every ID used in Super User and additionally asserted each of its own IDs present in Loop's source file, and Ideas excluded every ID used in either. Cross-round dedupe removed 27 already-published tweets, the highest count recorded, and a date filter applied after it removed a further 14 posts dated outside the window, which fast mode's cached tier had returned despite the date parameters. Two collection bugs were found and written into the prompt after backup: a Loop keyword with quotes around only half the phrase, which made xpoz treat the space as OR and match the bare word "agent" - 100 rows of NFL free agents and foreign agents, all collapsed onto a single day; and fast mode's undocumented 100-row ceiling, which returns exactly 100 and gives no truncation warning, caught by splitting an OR group into its three phrases and getting 101 back. Job Scanner: 28 boards scanned, 24 OK, 40 in-window postings, 10 dupes skipped, 30 published as EN+ZH pairs, 0 failures; the usual 4 slugs 404'd. Collection ran through four parallel subagents; the CSV export quota had reset with the month, so both hot keywords pulled cleanly in one request each.

Plan: The Reddit dedupe work is now correctly sequenced and blocked on a schema change, so until that lands the next run should still expect to remove 5-10 Reddit repeats by hand and should log the count. Second, act on the changed division of labour rather than observing it again: the next Ideas run should read the Twitter set explicitly for themes and judgement and the Reddit set explicitly for product gaps, and should stop scoring the two against the same bar. Third, the two collection bugs found this run were both silent, which raises the obvious question of what else is being lost without an error - the next run should deliberately check whether any other OR group returns exactly 100 rows. The 35-run zero-approval backlog at 48 pending remains the top standing blocker, and two of those pending items are now prerequisites for each other, which is a new kind of cost the queue is imposing.
