# Google gave the family AI agent its very own Google account

> 来源 / Source: https://clauday.com/article/7c94c71d-9280-4b54-b3b7-a8a582938c97

Google opened CC to households on September 18. Up to six people share one agent, and the detail that makes it more than a feature release is that the agent gets its own Google account. Not a delegated scope on yours. Its own identity, with its own calendar access, its own document access, its own presence in the sharing model. TechCrunch has the launch at https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/18/googles-new-cc-is-an-ai-agent-that-helps-families-run-their-households/.

What it does is deliberately unglamorous. A shared Your Day Ahead brief goes out every morning to everyone with access. It tracks dates and to-dos across the household. It fills out permission slips and activity registration PDFs, which is the kind of task that is trivially easy to describe and has defeated every assistant that came before because it requires reading a badly-scanned form, knowing your kid's date of birth, and committing to an answer. CC runs on Gemini models with the Antigravity harness and reaches into Maps, Docs and Sheets.

Giving an agent its own account is the interesting bet and it cuts both ways. It solves real problems: a shared agent acting as six different people has no coherent permission story, and every audit log becomes useless the moment you cannot tell whether the human or the agent touched a document. A separate identity fixes both. It also creates a principal that six people can instruct, that holds credentials none of them individually granted, and whose actions are attributable to an entity that cannot be held responsible for anything. Family software is where that gets tested first, because the trust boundaries inside a household are informal, asymmetric, and nobody reads the permission dialog.

US only, 18 and up, personal Google accounts, existing users get upgrade prompts and everyone else goes on a waitlist. The privacy documentation is reportedly still incomplete, which for a product whose entire premise is an agent-owned account reading six people's calendars is the thing to press on. Watch whether Apple and Microsoft follow with agent identities of their own, because if they do, the assistant stops being a feature of your account and becomes an account you have to administer.
