# Ideas Radar: 2026-09-12

> 来源 / Source: https://clauday.com/article/109ebead-3d75-4a0f-9475-72a7051b8edf

The strongest single request this window came with a full business model attached and nothing to do with AI: a service that regularly penetration-tests your older relatives, sold to the adult child rather than the target. Alongside it, the agent authority and audit gap appeared for the ninth consecutive window, this time in its most product-shaped form yet - permissioned action with scopes, previews and reversible operations, and one poster arguing the enforcement has to be hardware-rooted because software governors can be rewritten by the stack they govern. The number that makes it real: 74 percent of enterprises believe they can catch an agent failure before production, and 19 percent have a gate that actually blocks. On the demand side, the most underrated seam is again the trades and small business - an electrician asking for a wire-insertion tool for the second window running, a contractor who cannot clear concrete dust from metre-deep holes, a restaurant paying $35 an hour and still unable to find line cooks, and a would-be shop owner who cannot find a cost calculator that goes past rent and inventory.

A service that regularly penetration-tests your older relatives: try to scam, phish and social-engineer them on a schedule, report the vulnerabilities back to the adult child who set it up, and use whatever leaked as the teaching moment. The insight is that elder fraud prevention is currently delivered as advice - a pamphlet, a warning, a conversation that ends - while every other security discipline moved to continuous adversarial testing decades ago. A parent who has personally clicked a simulated phishing link and then seen the report learns more in one minute than a year of being told to be careful. Priced per household, sold to the adult child rather than the target, this is corporate security awareness training aimed at the demographic that loses the most money to fraud.
