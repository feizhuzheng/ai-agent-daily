# There's a Grey Market for AI Credits and It's Bigger Than You Think

> Source: [clauday.com](https://clauday.com/article/ed0416f2-6abd-4f01-b05b-ee35a593e042)

Somebody finally went and mapped the AI credit resale market, and the writeup hit Hacker News' front page this weekend. Short version: unused OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and Microsoft credits are being resold at 30 to 80 percent off list, and there is enough of it to matter.

The supply side is startup credits. Every accelerator and cloud program hands out five and six figures of API credit that most companies never burn through. That used to get swapped informally in founder Slacks. It's now a market with tiers. Direct brokers who pool keys and resell access — one claimed capacity for $100,000 of spend per day. Actual marketplaces like AI Credits and AICreditMart. Routers such as CheapCredits, Tokvana and Neokens that advertise "bulk pricing," which the author suspects is brokered credit wearing a nicer shirt. And under all of it, Telegram groups, r/saasforsale, r/indiehackers. Listings observed included $200,000 of OpenAI credit and $10,000 of Anthropic.

Note the structure on the direct brokers: they don't hand you the key, they proxy your calls through a pooled key. Which means your prompts, your data and your agent traffic all run through a stranger's infrastructure. If you're wondering why an agent stack would ever route through that — the answer is a 70% discount and a founder watching runway.

The line worth stealing from the piece: "Tokens have become a pseudo-currency, and there is enough liquidity in the market to allow for a lot of abuse." That's the real finding. Credits were designed as a marketing subsidy. They now trade, clear, and have a spread.

This connects to something we've written about repeatedly — nobody can independently verify what an agent run costs. Turns out that's not just an accounting problem.
